Next-gen laptops set to launch this year

This promises to be an interesting year for buyers of business class notebooks, with all three of the big vendors—Dell, HP, and Lenovo—showing major changes to their lineups, and the big chip vendors offering new options that promise better performance and manageability. Many of these were shown at CES 2025 in Las Vegas, and I'll detail some highlights from the big vendors below, but first some general thoughts. I've been impressed by the improvements in performance and battery life in the current generation of laptops powered by the latest chips from AMD, Intel, and Qualcomm. Compared with the machines I tested just a year ago, the improvements are noticeable. Qualcomm's Snapdragon X Elite chips are good performers on native-ARM applications like the Microsoft Office suite (even if they have compatibility issues with some enterprise applications), while offering the best battery life I've seen. And while I and most of the other reviewers generally test performance when plugged in, Qualcomm argues that performance doesn't degrade as much on battery life. Meanwhile, both systems with AMD's Ryzen AI 300 ("Strix Point") or Intel's Core Ultra 200V ("Lunar Lake") are showing not only better performance than their previous generations, but also notably better battery life as well. That's a win for everyone.
